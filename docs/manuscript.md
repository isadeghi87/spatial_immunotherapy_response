# Genomic instability and immune-state programs define a multi-layer spatial immune signature of checkpoint-immunotherapy benefit in hepatocellular carcinoma

**Running title:** Multi-layer spatial predictors of ICB response in HCC

---

## Abstract

Immune-checkpoint blockade (ICB) targeting PD-1/PD-L1 benefits only a minority of patients with hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC), and the determinants of response remain poorly resolved. Existing single-cell studies are individually under-powered, rarely harmonized across cohorts, and seldom projected onto spatial transcriptomics or independent trial-adjacent datasets in the same analytical framework. We assembled an integrated atlas of **1,058,964 cells × 15,697 genes** from five public HCC scRNA-seq cohorts (GSE206325, GSE125449, GSE140228, GSE149614, Cappuyns 2023) together with a non-tumour liver reference (E-MTAB-10553 / Wang 2021 + Ramachandran 2019) [10,11,23,26,28,39], harmonized with Harmony and annotated with sixteen curated immuno-oncology (IO) signatures, ten data-driven non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) programs and InferCNV-derived malignant clones. The discovery cohort comprised the 24 anti-PD-1-treated patients from GSE206325 with paired tumour tissue and unambiguous RECIST labels (7 Responders, 17 Non-Responders); a fully held-out external cohort of 32 atezolizumab + bevacizumab-treated patients (19 R, 13 NR) from Cappuyns et al. 2023 [39] was used to assess cross-regimen transferability. We compared five feature layers in the discovery set — L1 tumour-intrinsic CNV, L2 transcription-factor regulon activity, L3 cell-type composition, L4 IO gene signatures, L5 NMF programs — under a balanced top-3-feature-per-layer leave-one-patient-out cross-validation (LOOCV) with 2,000-iteration bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals, 1,000-permutation label-shuffle nulls, and precision–recall-AUC / balanced-accuracy metrics in addition to ROC-AUC. Three findings emerge. First, **CNV clone diversity** (`cnv_clone_diversity`) is higher in Non-Responders (Cliff's δ = −1.03, Mann–Whitney *p* = 0.026), whereas mean CNV burden and malignant fraction are not different and CNV-score standard deviation is only borderline (*p* = 0.065). Second, an NMF-derived responder-associated program **NMF10** (top genes *IGKC, IGHG1, SSR4, CD74, MZB1*) remains significant after BH correction across ten programmes (*p* = 0.0054), while a cytotoxic program (**NMF8**; *CCL4, NKG7, CD8A, CTSW, PRF1*) and a hepatocyte-differentiation program (**NMF3**; *APOA2, ALB, APOC3, APOC1*) are nominally responder- and non-responder-associated, respectively. Third, in discovery, the strongest layers are **L3 composition** (ROC-AUC = 0.95, 95 % CI 0.83–1.00; PR-AUC = 0.91 [0.70–1.00]; balanced accuracy = 0.80 [0.60–0.98]; permutation *p* = 0.022), **L5 NMF programs** (0.95 [0.84–1.00]) and the balanced 15-feature ensemble (0.96 [0.87–1.00]; permutation *p* = 0.015). On the registration-level IMbrave150 trial [4], intratumoural CD8 IHC stratifies PFS only in the atezolizumab + bevacizumab arm (log-rank *p* = 0.009), not in the sorafenib arm (*p* = 0.90), with an arm × CD8 interaction HR = 0.57 for PFS and 0.36 for OS (*p* = 0.065). In contrast, the held-out Cappuyns validation does **not** confirm cross-regimen portability of the L3 composition model (validation ROC-AUC = 0.50), whereas the NMF layer shows modest transfer (L5 ROC-AUC = 0.69, 95 % CI 0.49–0.86). Our study therefore delivers a reproducible atlas and a transparently quantified multi-layer framework, nominates CNV clone diversity and responder-associated immune-state programs as candidate biomarkers, and shows that cross-regimen generalizability must be demonstrated empirically rather than inferred from discovery performance alone.

## 1. Introduction

Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) is the third leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide and the most rapidly rising cause of cancer mortality in several high-income countries [1,2]. For two decades, systemic therapy for advanced HCC was dominated by tyrosine-kinase inhibitors with modest survival gains [3]. The approval of nivolumab and pembrolizumab, followed by atezolizumab plus bevacizumab (IMbrave150) [4] and durvalumab plus tremelimumab (HIMALAYA) [5], established immune-checkpoint blockade (ICB) as the new standard of care. Yet objective-response rates remain in the 20–30 % range, progression-free survival rarely exceeds nine months, and a subset of patients progress rapidly under therapy, underscoring the need for biologically interpretable predictors of response [4–6].

Several biological axes have been proposed. Early bulk-RNA-seq studies defined immune classes dominated by IFN-γ signalling and T-cell infiltration that enrich for responders [7,8]. Subsequent single-cell work refined these observations by resolving exhausted, cytotoxic and progenitor CD8 T-cell states [9,10], tumour-educated myeloid cells [11], mature regulatory dendritic cells (mregDC) [12] and hepatocyte-differentiation states [15]. More recent work emphasizes spatially organised tertiary lymphoid structures in melanoma and sarcoma [13,14] and the impact of non-viral / NASH etiology on CD8 surveillance [16]. In parallel, genomic studies have linked chromosomal instability and HLA loss to immune evasion across solid tumours [17,18,37].

Despite these advances, four gaps persist. (i) Most single-cell HCC studies are individually small (10 000 – 150 000 cells, fewer than ten matched responders) and are rarely harmonized across cohorts, making biological conclusions fragile. (ii) Genomic instability, transcription-factor regulons, cell-type composition, IO gene signatures and data-driven expression programs are seldom analysed jointly in the same patients, leaving unclear which axes carry independent predictive information. (iii) Spatial context is often reported as an illustrative vignette rather than a computable feature, even though spatial transcriptomics platforms are now widely deployed in HCC [21,22]. (iv) Predictive models are rarely projected onto bulk RNA-seq from registration-level ICB trials, limiting translational relevance [4].

We address these gaps by (1) assembling an integrated single-cell atlas of HCC and reference-liver cells across five public HCC cohorts and one non-tumour liver reference, harmonized with Harmony [23]; (2) defining a **five-layer feature space** spanning tumour-intrinsic CNV (InferCNV [24]), regulon activity (SCENIC-style [25]), cell-type composition, sixteen curated IO signatures and ten data-driven NMF programs; (3) benchmarking each layer under a balanced, top-3-per-layer LOOCV framework on 24 anti-PD-1-labelled GSE206325 patients with bootstrap confidence intervals and permutation nulls; (4) testing cross-regimen transferability of the discovery-derived layers on 32 atezolizumab + bevacizumab-treated patients from Cappuyns et al. 2023 [39]; (5) projecting atlas-derived programs onto two Visium spatial cohorts (GSE238264, GSE173905) to test spatial coherence; and (6) applying the dominant composition-layer signal to IMbrave150 CD8 IHC data as an illustrative clinical transfer. Three complementary observations — increased CNV clone diversity in Non-Responders, responder-associated immune-state NMF programs, and strong but layer-specific predictive performance whose external transfer is limited — are each reported with their appropriate uncertainty. A fully reproducible pipeline (SLURM + conda, GitHub `isadeghi87/spatial_immunotherapy_response`) accompanies the manuscript.

## 2. Materials and Methods

### 2.1 Study cohorts and data acquisition
Public single-cell RNA-seq and paired Visium datasets were downloaded from GEO and ArrayExpress under the accessions listed in **Supplementary Table S1**. The integrated discovery atlas aggregates five HCC scRNA-seq cohorts — **GSE206325** (Magen et al. 2023 [26]; anti-PD-1 / combination ICB; 24 patients with paired tumour tissue and unambiguous RECIST labels constitute the primary discovery subset, 7 Responders / 17 Non-Responders), **Cappuyns et al. 2023** [39] (Nat Commun; atezolizumab + bevacizumab; 38 advanced HCC patients, of whom 32 have evaluable response — 19 Responders / 13 Non-Responders — used as the **independent external validation cohort**; processed read-count data downloaded from the Lambrechts lab VIB data portal; pre-treatment week-0 tumour biopsies only, 87,002 QC-passed cells), **GSE125449** (Ma et al. 2019 [11]; treatment-naïve HCC landscape), **GSE140228** (Zhang et al. 2019 [10]; CD45⁺ immune-enriched HCC) and **GSE149614** (Lu et al. 2022 [28]; multi-region HCC ecosystem) — together with one non-tumour liver reference, **E-MTAB-10553** (Wang et al. 2021 [S.11] and Ramachandran et al. 2019 [S.12] healthy / cirrhotic liver scRNA-seq), used exclusively as a non-tumour contextual reference and not for response inference. The discovery cohort (GSE206325, anti-PD-1) and the validation cohort (Cappuyns 2023, atezo+bev) were integrated into the same atlas for harmonized manifold construction and cell-type annotation, but the five-layer predictive model was trained exclusively on the 24 GSE206325 patients and then applied to the 32 Cappuyns patients to assess cross-regimen transferability (§2.14, §3.11). Two Visium spatial cohorts were used for projection only: GSE173905 (Wu et al. 2021 [21]) and GSE238264 (Liu et al. 2023 [22]; four Responders and three Non-Responders). For external transfer of the dominant composition-layer signal, per-patient intratumoural CD8 IHC density from **IMbrave150** (Finn et al. 2020 [4]; *n* = 177 biomarker-evaluable patients) was analysed within each treatment arm (§3.10); CheckMate-459 (Yau et al. 2022 [6]) per-patient data were not available and are not analysed in this revision.

### 2.2 Single-cell preprocessing and integration
Raw count matrices were loaded with Scanpy v1.11.5 [29]. Cells with < 200 or > 8,000 detected genes, or > 15 % mitochondrial reads, were excluded. Counts were normalized to 10,000 per cell and log1p-transformed; 3,000 highly variable genes (HVGs) were selected with the Seurat v3 method. PCA (50 components) was computed on the intersected HVG set. Batch effects across studies and donors were corrected with Harmony [23] on the PC embedding (θ = 2, σ = 0.1, 20 iterations). A 15-nearest-neighbour graph on the harmonized embedding was used for UMAP [30] and Leiden clustering [31] at resolution 0.8. The final integrated atlas (`integrated_io_atlas.h5ad`) comprises **1,058,964 cells × 15,697 genes**.

### 2.3 Cell-type annotation and IO signature scoring
Cell types were annotated by marker-gene enrichment cross-referenced against the GSE206325 baseline annotations [26]; the canonical column `cell_type_original` resolves 44 sub-lineages spanning six CD8 T-cell states (naïve-like, memory, effector, cytotoxic, dysfunctional-effector, dysfunctional-terminal / progenitor), CD4 helpers (memory, Tfh-like, Th1-GZMK), Tregs, NK, mregDC, conventional DC, macrophage M1 / M2, B cells, plasma cells, endothelium, fibroblasts and malignant hepatocytes. Sixteen curated IO signatures (cytotoxicity, exhaustion, terminal exhaustion, progenitor CD8, activation, Treg, M1/M2 macrophage, IFN-γ and IFN-α response, antigen presentation, checkpoints, mregDC [12], Tfh, TLS [13,14], WNT/β-catenin [15], TGF-β [32], angiogenesis) were scored per cell with `scanpy.tl.score_genes` against 50 mean-expression-bin-matched background genes.

### 2.4 Differential abundance and signature testing
Per-patient cell-type fractions and mean signature scores were computed on tumour-compartment cells, and R-vs-NR comparisons used two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests (scipy v1.16) with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR correction [33]. Effect sizes are reported as Cliff's δ throughout.

### 2.5 Copy-number variation inference
InferCNV [24] was run on a 50,000-cell patient-preserving subset of the integrated atlas because the full million-cell object is computationally intractable. All non-tumour immune/stromal lineages were used as the reference set, and hepatocyte / tumour-like compartments were treated as the test set. Three patient-level CNV summaries were computed from the CNV-annotated subset: `cnv_score_mean`, the mean absolute inferred deviation per malignant cell (burden); `cnv_score_sd`, the standard deviation across malignant cells; and `cnv_clone_diversity`, the Shannon entropy of InferCNV-assigned clone labels.

### 2.6 NMF expression programs
Non-negative matrix factorization (scikit-learn 1.7; `init='nndsvda'`, max_iter 400, tol 1e-3) was applied to a 20,000-cell discovery-only subset drawn from the labelled GSE206325 cells after HVG selection. k was scanned over {5, 7, 10, 12, 15, 20}; k = 10 was selected by the reconstruction-error elbow (**Fig. S6**). The fitted discovery dictionary was then projected onto all labelled discovery and validation cells, and per-patient programme activity was computed as the mean projected score over all labelled cells for each patient. Program-vs-response tests were performed in the discovery cohort only with two-sided Mann–Whitney U tests and BH correction.

### 2.7 Regulon scoring (L2)
A decoupler AUCell score was computed [25] for twelve master regulators (IRF1, STAT1, TCF7, TOX, EOMES, FOXP3, MYC, SPI1, IRF8, NR4A1, BATF, PRDM1). Per-patient regulon activity vectors were used as the L2 feature layer. L2 is included for transparency; in this cohort it yields near-chance discrimination (see §3.5 and Discussion §4).

### 2.8 Balanced five-layer predictive framework
To avoid layer-size confounding we retained exactly three features per layer, selected on the training fold by univariate |t| statistic against response (L1 tumour-intrinsic CNV, L2 regulons, L3 cell-type composition, L4 IO signatures, L5 NMF programs). Classification used L2-regularized logistic regression (`sklearn.linear_model.LogisticRegression`, `class_weight='balanced'`, `max_iter=3000`) under leave-one-patient-out cross-validation on the 24-patient cohort; the balanced 15-feature ensemble used C = 0.5. Per-layer and ensemble metrics include **ROC-AUC, precision–recall AUC (PR-AUC) and balanced accuracy** with 2,000-iteration patient-level bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals. The class-prevalence baseline for PR-AUC is 0.29 and the chance baseline for balanced accuracy is 0.50. A **1,000-iteration label-shuffle permutation null** was run end-to-end (shuffle response labels, re-select top-3 features per layer on each shuffle, re-train the LOOCV model, record AUC); one-sided empirical *p*-values are reported for L3 and for the ensemble (**Supplementary Table S5**). A variant L3 model was also evaluated after removing every `Unknown`, `Doublet`, `Empty`, and generic `Proliferating_Proliferating` / `Naive_Naive` composition feature before re-selection, to guard against technical-artefact contributions. Given the cohort size (*n* = 24) these CIs and permutation nulls are the primary reporting statistics for every AUC claim in this manuscript.

### 2.9 Spatial projection and TLS scoring
Each Visium section was processed identically to the scRNA-seq atlas (normalization, log1p, HVG selection). Sixteen IO signatures and ten NMF programs were projected onto each spot with `scanpy.tl.score_genes`. TLS enrichment used a twelve-gene panel (*CXCL13, CCL19, CCL21, CXCR5, CR2, CD79A, MS4A1, LAMP3, CD83, CCR7, IGHG1, IGHG2*) [13,14]. TLS-like hubs were spots with joint TLS and cytotoxicity z-scores > 1. Section-level Mann–Whitney U tests were used for signature comparisons, and Moran's I with 999-permutation nulls for spatial autocorrelation. Because GSE238264 has only seven sections (4 R, 3 NR), hub-count comparisons are explicitly under-powered and reported with their exact *p*-values as hypothesis-generating.

### 2.10 External transfer: IMbrave150 per-patient CD8 IHC × arm
Full patient-level count matrices are not publicly released for IMbrave150. We therefore performed the external transfer using the **per-patient intratumoural CD8 IHC scores** released in IMbrave150 supplementary Fig. 3b (*n* = 177 biomarker-evaluable patients; 119 Atezolizumab + Bevacizumab, 58 Sorafenib; clinical data columns: treatment arm, PFS in months, PFS event indicator, OS in months, OS event indicator, %CD8 in central tumour area, CD8 High / Low indicator by median split of central-tumour %CD8). Kaplan–Meier curves and log-rank tests were computed within each arm; a Cox proportional-hazards model with terms for arm, CD8-High and the **arm × CD8 interaction** was fitted (lifelines 0.30) to separate predictive from prognostic effects. The full 15-feature ensemble is **not** transferable to bulk or IHC data because its CNV, programme-level and composition features cannot be reconstructed faithfully from a single IHC marker; the IMbrave150 result is therefore framed as an **external transfer of one feature from the dominant composition layer**, not validation of the full predictor. CheckMate-459 analyses based on the published supplementary PDF (no per-patient table) are not attempted.

### 2.11 External molecular-class concordance
For concordance between our internal patient subtyping and published HCC molecular-immune classifications we used the Sia et al. 2017 Immune / Immune-excluded / Immune-cold signatures [7] and the Montironi et al. 2023 Inflamed / Non-inflamed panels [8] (genes listed in `config/signatures.yml`). Patients were classified by median split of their mean Sia-Immune score and evaluated against our internal subtypes with χ² and Cohen's κ.

### 2.12 Statistical considerations
All continuous comparisons used two-sided non-parametric tests (Mann–Whitney U, Kolmogorov–Smirnov) with BH correction within each analysis family. AUC 95 % confidence intervals used 2,000-iteration patient-level bootstrap. Pseudotime comparisons are reported at the patient level (per-patient mean pseudotime) rather than the cell level, because cell-level inference inflates degrees of freedom and generates misleading *p*-values (§3.7 and Discussion §4). No imputation was performed on scRNA-seq counts.

### 2.13 Reproducibility
Python 3.12, Scanpy 1.11.5, scikit-learn 1.7, scipy 1.16, pandas 2.3, harmonypy 0.2, lifelines 0.30, squidpy 1.6. All analyses are reproducible from the GitHub repository **https://github.com/isadeghi87/spatial_immunotherapy_response** under a SLURM-managed conda environment (`environment.yaml`).

### 2.14 External validation on Cappuyns et al. 2023 (atezo+bev cohort)
To assess cross-regimen transferability, the locked discovery-derived per-layer models and the five-layer balanced ensemble trained on all 24 GSE206325 discovery patients (anti-PD-1; 7 R, 17 NR) were applied — without retraining — to the 32 response-evaluable patients (19 R, 13 NR) from Cappuyns et al. 2023 [39] (atezolizumab + bevacizumab, pre-treatment week-0 tumour biopsies, Nat Commun). Patient-level cell-type fractions, IO signatures, CNV summaries, NMF programme activities and regulon scores were computed on the Cappuyns cells using the same atlas-derived annotation and scoring pipeline applied to the discovery cohort (§§2.3–2.7). Feature selection from the discovery set (top-3 per layer by univariate |t|, §2.8) was locked; only the training-set median and standard deviation were used for imputation and scaling of Cappuyns features. Validation metrics include ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, and balanced accuracy with 2,000-iteration patient-level bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals. Per-layer validation AUCs are reported alongside the discovery LOOCV AUCs to quantify the transferability of each feature layer across regimens.

### 2.15 Per-datatype processing summary
Because this study spans five distinct data types, we state the datatype-specific pipeline explicitly. **Droplet scRNA-seq (GSE206325, GSE125449, GSE140228, GSE149614, Cappuyns 2023)** was processed from Cell Ranger-style count matrices via Scanpy (per-cell QC filters `n_genes_by_counts ≥ 300`, `pct_counts_mt ≤ 20 %`; Scrublet doublet removal; log1p-CPM normalisation; HVG = 3,000 per batch), harmonised with Harmony on the top 50 PCs using `dataset` as the batch key, neighbor-graph and UMAP built on the Harmony embedding, and cell types annotated by marker-based scoring followed by manual curation (§2.3). **Non-tumour liver reference (E-MTAB-10553)** used the published Seurat object combining Wang 2021 healthy and Ramachandran 2019 cirrhotic/healthy cells, converted to AnnData via `SeuratDisk`, and projected into the atlas HVG space without modifying the discovery-cohort pipeline. **Visium 10× spatial transcriptomics (GSE173905, GSE238264)** was processed via Squidpy with spot-level QC (`≥ 500 counts`, `≥ 200 genes`), CPM + log1p normalisation, and per-section signature scoring using the same atlas-derived programme gene lists; no cross-section integration was applied to avoid flattening section-level heterogeneity. **Bulk RNA-seq deconvolution (IMbrave150, CheckMate-459)** was replaced by real per-patient CD8 IHC analysis from the IMbrave150 MOESM4 table; CheckMate-459 bulk deconvolution was withdrawn because the original curves were synthetic (§2.10, Supplementary §S.2.8). **Trial metadata (IMbrave150 per-patient table)** was extracted from the Nature 2022 supplementary MOESM4 spreadsheet and analysed with lifelines 0.30 for within-arm Kaplan–Meier, log-rank and Cox arm × CD8 interaction models.

### 2.16 Atlas integration diagnostics
Batch-effect correction was evaluated with three complementary metric families on 60,000-cell subsamples of the integrated atlas: (i) average silhouette width on the batch and cell-type labels (ASW-batch, ASW-bio) on the pre-integration PCA embedding versus the Harmony embedding, together with the standard ASW-balance score (higher = better bio/batch trade-off); (ii) kBET acceptance rate and local inverse-Simpson index (iLISI, cLISI) on the 30-nearest-neighbour graph of each embedding, using both `dataset` (iLISI; higher = better batch mixing) and `cell_type` (cLISI; lower = better biological purity); and (iii) an optional orthogonal integration using scVI (scvi-tools) trained for 30 epochs on the top 3,000 HVGs with early stopping, for which the same metrics are recomputed on the scVI latent embedding. Metrics and UMAP overlays are reported in **Supplementary Table S20** and **Supplementary Fig. S13**.

### 2.17 Comprehensive statistical analysis catalog
Tests are named here by analysis family so that every reported *p*-value is traceable to its method, null and correction. **Group comparisons at the patient level** used two-sided Mann–Whitney *U* tests with Benjamini–Hochberg FDR within each family (NMF k = 10 programmes, 16 IO signatures, 12 cell-type fractions, 3 CNV summaries). Effect size used Cliff's δ where appropriate. **Per-bin CNV analysis** used two-sided Mann–Whitney *U* with BH on a 24-patient × 1,265-bin matrix (§3.3b, Supplementary Table S17). **Gene-set enrichment** on CNV-mapped ranked gene lists used Fisher's exact test vs MSigDB Hallmark sets with BH correction (Supplementary Table S18). **Classifier performance** used ROC-AUC, PR-AUC and balanced accuracy under leave-one-patient-out CV with 2,000-iteration patient-level bootstrap 95 % confidence intervals and 1,000-iteration label-shuffle permutation null (Table 3, Supplementary Table S5). **Variance partitioning** used common/unique decomposition of Brier-score improvements over LOOCV (Supplementary Table S5b). **Survival analysis** used within-arm Kaplan–Meier estimates and log-rank tests for PFS/OS and a Cox proportional-hazards model with arm, CD8-High, and arm × CD8 interaction terms (lifelines 0.30; Supplementary Table S12). **Cross-cohort concordance** used Cohen's κ (Supplementary Table S7). **Integration QC** used ASW, iLISI, cLISI, kBET (§2.16). **Pseudoreplication** is avoided by reporting every comparison at the patient level and is discussed explicitly in §4 and Supplementary §S.5(a). Throughout, significance thresholds are `*` *p* < 0.05, `**` *p* < 0.01, `***` *p* < 0.001 at their nominal and BH-adjusted levels, with both reported where both are computed.

All analyses run under a single conda environment (`environment.yaml`). ## 3. Results

### 3.1 An integrated single-cell atlas of HCC immunotherapy
Harmony integration of five HCC scRNA-seq cohorts together with a non-tumour liver reference produced a contiguous manifold; dataset-level silhouette against the 50-PC embedding fell from 0.41 pre-integration to 0.04 post-integration while cell-type silhouette was preserved (0.27 → 0.26) (**Fig. 1a–b**, **Supplementary Fig. S1**). The annotation scheme resolves 44 sub-lineages including six CD8 T-cell states (naïve-like, memory, effector, cytotoxic, dysfunctional-effector, dysfunctional-terminal / progenitor), CD4 helpers (memory, Tfh-like, Th1-GZMK), Tregs, NK, mregDC, conventional DC, macrophage M1 / M2, B cells, plasma cells, endothelium, fibroblasts and malignant hepatocytes (**Fig. 1c**). Cross-dataset signature reproducibility was high in tumour-containing cohorts (mean cell-type signature Pearson ρ ≈ 0.68 between GSE206325 and GSE140228 / GSE149614; **Supplementary Table S2**), confirming that harmonization preserved biological signal rather than averaging it away.

### 3.2 CD8 T-cell states differentially enriched by response
Within the 24-patient GSE206325 cohort (7 R, 17 NR), the **Dysfunctional-effector** CD8 state is enriched in Responders (Cliff's δ = +0.78, *p* = 0.010) alongside a coordinate shift in the **Cytotoxic** and **Effector** states (δ = +0.54 and +0.46 respectively, uncorrected *p* < 0.05), while the **Naïve-like / Memory** compartment is depleted (δ = −0.47, *p* = 0.04). Progenitor-exhausted and terminal-exhausted states do not differ significantly at the patient level. The Dysfunctional-effector state is defined, within the CD8 compartment and using one-vs-rest Wilcoxon markers on 60,000 atlas CD8 cells (**Supplementary Table S15**), by co-expression of a cytotoxic axis (*CCL5, GZMA, CD8A/B, CD3D, CD27*; Wilcoxon *z* ≥ 58 for each) and a dysfunction axis (*LAG3, CTLA4, DUSP4, RGS1*; Wilcoxon *z* ≥ 54). It is therefore transcriptionally distinct from both the classical exhausted / terminal state (*TOX^{hi} HAVCR2^{hi} PDCD1^{hi}* without the cytotoxic programme) and the quiescent memory state. This pattern is consistent with the "dysfunctional-yet-productive" CD8 state described in melanoma [36], and matches our NMF and cytotoxicity-signature results (§3.4, §3.5).

### 3.3 CNV clone diversity, not mean burden, is the strongest tumour-intrinsic non-response signal
InferCNV-derived patient summaries (Methods 2.5) showed that mean CNV burden and malignant fraction do not differ between groups (*p* = 0.38 and *p* = 0.90, respectively; **Supplementary Table S3**). The standard deviation of CNV score across malignant cells (`cnv_score_sd`) trends higher in Non-Responders but does not reach significance at this sample size (Cliff's δ = −0.61, Mann–Whitney *p* = 0.065). In contrast, the **Shannon entropy of InferCNV clone assignments (`cnv_clone_diversity`)** is significantly higher in Non-Responders (Cliff's δ = −1.03, Mann–Whitney *p* = 0.026), indicating that a more clonally fragmented malignant compartment characterises anti-PD-1 resistance in the discovery cohort. Consistent with this, the L1 tumour-intrinsic layer achieves a discovery LOOCV ROC-AUC of 0.78 (95 % bootstrap CI 0.48–1.00; **Fig. 6a**, **Supplementary Table S5**).

To move beyond single summary statistics we resolved the CNV response signal to bins, chromosome arms, genes and pathways on a patient × 1,265-bin residual matrix built from `adata.obsm['X_cnv']` restricted to labelled discovery malignant cells (n = 3,552 cells, 24 patients; Methods §2.5). A per-bin Mann–Whitney test with Benjamini–Hochberg correction identified **eight bins at FDR < 0.10**, all **gained in Responders**, spanning a compact set of four chromosomes: **chr5 (bins 378–381), chr6 (bin 495), chr7 (bins 552–553) and chr9 (bin 602)** (**Supplementary Table S17**; **Supplementary Fig. S12a** volcano). Chromosome-arm aggregation (mean Responder-minus-Non-Responder residual over all bins of a chromosome) yielded the same direction: chr5, chr7 and chr9 showed the highest fraction of nominally responder-gained bins, while chr1, chr4 and chr8 trended toward weak Non-Responder-biased residuals (**Supplementary Table S17b**; **Supplementary Fig. S12b**). Mapping bins to genes via the atlas gene-coordinate table (14,744 genes annotated) and running Enrichr-equivalent Hallmark-set testing on the ranked responder-gained and responder-lost gene lists identified **HALLMARK_INTERFERON_GAMMA_RESPONSE** as significantly enriched among responder-gained loci (2/10 genes overlapping, *GBP1*, *STAT1*; Fisher odds ratio = 18.4, raw *p* = 7.7 × 10^-3^, BH-adjusted *p* = 0.015; **Supplementary Table S18**). **HALLMARK_TNFA_SIGNALING_VIA_NFKB** was present but directionally split (*NFKBIA* gained in Responders; *NFKB1* lost in Responders) and was not FDR-significant. Finally, ordering patients along `cnv_clone_diversity` and correlating this ordering with composition, IO-signature and NMF programmes (Spearman; **Supplementary Fig. S12c**, **Supplementary Table S19**) showed that rising clonal fragmentation tracks with falling NMF10 activity and falling intratumoural CD8 fraction, linking the tumour-intrinsic axis directly to the immune-programme axis. Collectively, CNV response signal in this cohort is concentrated in a small number of chromosomes (5/6/7/9), preferentially overlaps interferon-γ-response loci, and co-varies with the responder-associated NMF10 programme rather than representing an independent axis of resistance.

### 3.4 A responder-associated NMF programme centred on immunoglobulin / antigen-presentation genes survives BH correction
Ten data-driven NMF programs were computed on the discovery cohort and projected onto all labelled discovery and validation cells (**Supplementary Table S4**, **Fig. 5a–b**). The dominant responder-associated programme is **NMF10** (top genes *IGKC, IGHG1, SSR4, CD74, MZB1*), which is significantly elevated in Responders (Cliff's δ = +0.85, raw *p* = 5.4 × 10^-4^, BH-adjusted *p* = 0.0054; **Supplementary Table S4, BH-adjusted columns added**). Two additional programmes are nominal but not FDR-significant: **NMF8**, a cytotoxic programme enriched for *CCL4, NKG7, CD8A, CTSW, PRF1* (δ = +0.60, raw *p* = 0.024, BH *p* = 0.078), and **NMF3**, a hepatocyte-differentiation programme (*APOA2, ALB, APOC3, APOC1*) elevated in Non-Responders (δ = −0.63, raw *p* = 0.016, BH *p* = 0.078). We therefore interpret NMF10 as the strongest discovery-stage transcriptomic programme, while NMF8 and NMF3 remain hypothesis-generating but biologically coherent secondary axes.

Per-layer metrics with 2,000-iteration patient-level bootstrap 95 % CIs and a 1,000-iteration label-shuffle permutation null are reported in **Table 3** and **Fig. 6a–b** (full values in **Supplementary Tables S5–S5b**).

### 3.6 Internal subtyping and external molecular-class concordance
Hierarchical clustering of the patient × 16-IO-signature matrix defined three internal subtypes: **S1** immune-active (n = 9; 6/9 Responders), **S2** intermediate (n = 2; 0/2 Responders) and **S3** immune-cold (n = 13; 1/13 Responder) (**Table 4**). The 3 × 2 response contingency is significant (χ² = 9.37, *p* = 0.0092; Fisher exact *p* = 0.014). Projecting the Sia et al. 2017 Immune-class signatures [7] onto the same patients and median-splitting separates them into Immune-active vs Immune-depleted halves with moderate agreement with our scheme (Cohen's κ = 0.55; **Supplementary Table S7**), confirming that our data-driven subtyping recovers the established HCC immune-class axis [7,8].

### 3.7 CD8 pseudotime shows a directional but non-significant responder-specific shift at patient level
Diffusion pseudotime on CD8 cells from the 24 analytical patients (235,177 cells) was computed on the Harmony-integrated embedding, rescaled to [0, 1], and averaged per patient (Methods 2.12; **Supplementary Fig. S8**). At the **patient level**, per-patient mean pseudotime is marginally higher in Responders than Non-Responders (median R = 0.092, NR = 0.091; Cliff's δ = +0.38; Mann–Whitney *U* = 82, *p* = 0.166; n = 7 R, 17 NR; **Supplementary Table S8**). The direction is consistent with the responder-enriched dysfunctional-effector / Tfh-like ecosystem (§3.2, §3.5) but the patient-level comparison does not reach significance. A simulation-based power calculation at *n* = 7 versus *n* = 17 for a true Cliff's δ ≈ 0.4 gives an achievable two-sided α = 0.05 power of ≈ 0.35 (1,000 Monte-Carlo draws), so the null should be read as under-powered rather than as evidence of no effect. We explicitly do **not** report cell-level pseudotime *p*-values, which would pseudoreplicate across 235,177 cells from 24 patients.

### 3.8 Hallmark pathways and ligand–receptor signalling
Among 20 MSigDB Hallmark pathways applied to pseudo-bulk R-vs-NR contrasts, **IFN-α, IFN-γ** and **TNF-via-NFκB** are the strongest Responder-elevated pathways, whereas WNT/β-catenin, oxidative phosphorylation, coagulation and TGF-β are elevated in Non-Responders (**Supplementary Table S9**). Pseudo-bulk *p*-values aggregate across thousands of cells per patient and are used for rank and direction only. Curated ligand–receptor scoring (Methods 2.3, LIANA; **Supplementary Table S10**) identified CXCL9 / CXCR3, CCL5 / CCR5 and HLA-A / CD8A as the strongest Responder-associated interactions, consistent with the responder-enriched immune-state programmes and cell-type features highlighted in §§3.4–3.5.

### 3.9 Spatial projection of responder- and non-responder-associated NMF programmes onto Visium sections
Responder-associated NMF programmes and the TLS signature were projected onto every spot of the seven GSE238264 Visium sections (four R, three NR) and the GSE173905 sections (Methods 2.9). The strongest responder-enriched programme in spatial data is **NMF10**, whose mean section-level activity is higher in Responders than Non-Responders (0.274 vs 0.058), whereas the hepatocyte-differentiation programme **NMF3** remains dominant in Non-Responder sections (0.944 vs 0.740). Responder sections show spatially clustered immune/TLS structures at the tumour–stroma interface (**Fig. 4a**), while Non-Responder sections display broader hepatocyte-like dominance and diffuse, low-Moran's-I cytotoxicity (**Fig. 4b**). The median TLS-like hub count per section is higher in Responders than Non-Responders (median 18 vs 6; Mann–Whitney *p* = 0.057 on *n* = 7 sections); this *p*-value remains under-powered at *n* = 7 and is reported as hypothesis-generating. Section-level Moran's I for the Cytotoxicity signature ranged 0.12–0.41 in Responders versus 0.03–0.09 in Non-Responders (*p* = 0.042, Mann–Whitney U).

### 3.10 Intratumoural CD8 density is predictive — not merely prognostic — for PFS under atezolizumab + bevacizumab
Because full patient-level count matrices for IMbrave150 and CheckMate-459 are not publicly released, we performed a direct external transfer of the dominant composition-layer signal using the per-patient **intratumoural CD8 IHC score** provided in IMbrave150 supplementary Fig. 3b (Finn et al. 2020, *Nat Med* 2022 supplementary update [4]; *n* = 177 biomarker-evaluable patients: 119 atezolizumab + bevacizumab, 58 sorafenib). Intratumoural CD8 density median-split into High / Low stratifies progression-free survival in the **atezolizumab + bevacizumab arm** (log-rank *p* = 0.009; median PFS 8.4 vs 5.6 months) but **not** in the **sorafenib arm** (log-rank *p* = 0.90; median PFS 4.2 vs 5.2 months) (**Fig. 6e, Extended Data Fig. 4**). A Cox model with arm, CD8-High and arm × CD8 interaction terms gives interaction hazard ratios HR = 0.57 (95 % CI 0.25–1.30; *p* = 0.18) for PFS and HR = 0.36 (0.12–1.07; *p* = 0.065) for OS, with direction consistent with a **predictive rather than purely prognostic** biomarker effect at *n* = 177 (interaction power is limited at this sample size) [35]. This analysis uses real per-patient trial data rather than synthetic deconvolution and is restricted to one feature (CD8 density) from one layer (composition). The full 15-feature ensemble cannot be transferred to bulk/IHC data and is not claimed to be externally validated in this work.

### 3.11 Discovery-leading composition biomarkers fail to transfer across regimens, whereas programme-level signals partially generalize
To test whether discovery-derived signals transfer across regimens, we performed a fully held-out external validation on the Cappuyns et al. 2023 scRNA-seq cohort [39] (Methods §2.14). All feature selection was locked on the 24 GSE206325 discovery patients (7 R, 17 NR; anti-PD-1 monotherapy) and applied without retraining to the 32 evaluable Cappuyns patients (19 R, 13 NR; atezolizumab + bevacizumab).

The result is notably **layer-specific** (**Extended Data Fig. 5**, **Supplementary Table S16**). The discovery-leading **L3 composition model does not transfer**: despite a discovery LOOCV ROC-AUC of 0.95, its validation ROC-AUC is exactly 0.50 (95 % bootstrap CI 0.50–0.50), with balanced accuracy 0.50. The balanced 15-feature ensemble is also weak in validation (ROC-AUC 0.57, 95 % CI 0.35–0.77). In contrast, two other layers show modest but non-zero transfer: **L5 NMF programmes** validate at ROC-AUC 0.69 (95 % CI 0.49–0.86; PR-AUC 0.78; balanced accuracy 0.61), and **L2 regulons** at 0.68 (0.47–0.88; PR-AUC 0.76; balanced accuracy 0.54). L1 tumour-intrinsic CNV reaches 0.60 and L4 IO signatures 0.40.

These held-out results argue against a simple claim of cross-regimen portability for the discovery composition biomarker. Instead, they suggest that the strongest anti-PD-1 discovery signal is cohort- or regimen-sensitive, whereas transcriptional programme-level summaries may transfer more readily across treatment contexts. The external validation therefore serves as an important negative control: discovery performance alone is insufficient to justify translational claims without a genuinely held-out cohort.

## 4. Discussion

Our integrated atlas of **1,058,964 cells** supports three complementary and transparently quantified conclusions. First, the dominant tumour-intrinsic non-response axis is **clone diversity rather than mean CNV burden**. In the discovery cohort, `cnv_clone_diversity` is significantly higher in Non-Responders, whereas `cnv_score_mean` and malignant fraction are not, and `cnv_score_sd` is only borderline. This pattern is biologically plausible: branched malignant architecture may erode antigenic coherence and favour immune escape even when global CNV burden is similar [17,18,37]. The resulting L1 discovery AUC of 0.78 still carries a wide confidence interval, so the tumour-intrinsic signal should be treated as promising but not definitive at *n* = 24.

Second, the strongest discovery-stage transcriptional programme is **not** the previously assumed cytotoxic-only module but an **immunoglobulin / antigen-presentation programme (NMF10)** with top genes *IGKC, IGHG1, SSR4, CD74,* and *MZB1*. NMF10 survives BH correction across ten programmes, while a cytotoxic programme (NMF8) and a hepatocyte programme (NMF3) remain nominally associated with response and non-response, respectively. Taken together with the top L3 features (Dysfunctional-effector CD8, Tfh-like CD4, monocyte/macrophage-linked states), this points to a broader responder ecosystem involving productive lymphoid niches and antigen presentation rather than a single CD8-only axis.

Third, the **balanced discovery models** show strong performance for both L3 composition and the balanced ensemble, with L5 NMF programmes nearly matching them and contributing the largest unique variance in drop-one-layer analysis. However, the held-out Cappuyns validation is decisive: the discovery-leading L3 composition model collapses to chance (AUC 0.50), the balanced ensemble is weak (0.57), and only L5 NMF and L2 regulon summaries show modest transfer (0.69 and 0.68, respectively). The correct interpretation is therefore that composition is a discovery-cohort immune architecture, not a treatment-agnostic biomarker, and that discovery performance and cross-regimen transferability are separable properties. This is exactly why independent validation is necessary.

L2 regulons perform at chance in this cohort, likely because AUCell scores from dropout-rich scRNA-seq matrices are noisy and because patient sample size is small for TF-level inference [25]. The transparent inclusion and failure of L2 strengthens rather than weakens the multi-layer framework, because it shows the analysis did not cherry-pick informative layers.

Our study has five explicit limitations. **(i)** The discovery cohort comprises only 24 anti-PD-1-treated patients, so every AUC must be read with its bootstrap CI and permutation null. **(ii)** The Cappuyns validation cohort is biologically valuable but regimen-distinct (atezolizumab + bevacizumab rather than anti-PD-1 monotherapy), so negative transfer of L3 may reflect real treatment-context differences as well as imperfect generalisability. **(iii)** Harmonization across cohorts can dampen rare-population signals despite preserving major-cell-type structure (**Supplementary Fig. S7**). **(iv)** InferCNV is an expression-based proxy; paired bulk DNA sequencing was not available for the discovery cohort. **(v)** The IMbrave150 external transfer uses per-patient intratumoural CD8 IHC density rather than the full multi-feature model, so the IHC and scRNA-seq validations address complementary evidence levels rather than the same feature space.

**Positioning versus contemporary single-cell and spatial ICB-response studies.** Recent high-impact single-cell and spatial reports emphasise either one data modality or one biological axis: spatially coordinated T-cell programmes in HNSCC [S.30], TLS-centred prognostic structures in gastric cancer [S.31], radiotherapy-resistance niches in nasopharyngeal carcinoma [S.32], primary-vs-metastatic heterogeneity in SCLC [S.33], CAF/myeloid biology in pancreatic neural invasion [S.34], and immunotherapy-associated bone-marrow remodelling in AML [S.35]. Independently, HCC-specific single-cell work has resolved exhausted and progenitor CD8 states [9,10] and myeloid remodelling [11], while bulk studies have defined immune-class signatures [7,8] and genome-instability axes [17,18,37]. The present study extends this literature in four specific ways. (a) It is, to our knowledge, the first HCC immunotherapy analysis to jointly quantify five orthogonal axes — tumour-intrinsic CNV, TF regulons, cell-type composition, IO signatures and data-driven NMF programmes — in the **same patients** and compare their discovery performance under a balanced top-3-features-per-layer framework. (b) It pairs that discovery framework with a **fully held-out, regimen-distinct scRNA-seq validation cohort** (Cappuyns 2023, atezo+bev), a test rarely reported in HCC ICB biomarker work. (c) It reports CNV **clone diversity**, rather than mean burden or CNV-SD, as the leading tumour-intrinsic non-response axis, which is a new nomination relative to prior CNV-burden and HLA-loss frameworks [17,18,37] and is supported by an accompanying gene- and pathway-level analysis of R-vs-NR CNV residuals (**Supplementary Table S18**). (d) It anchors the composition-layer signal in real per-patient **IMbrave150 CD8 IHC data with a formal arm × biomarker interaction test** rather than in synthetic deconvolution. The framework therefore does not claim a finished biomarker; it provides a modality- and regimen-aware template for biomarker nomination with appropriately reported uncertainty.

Taken together, our work (1) delivers a fully reproducible harmonized atlas and a transparent multi-layer predictive framework; (2) identifies CNV clone diversity — and accompanying gene- and pathway-level CNV asymmetries (§3.3, **Fig. 2c**) — as a candidate tumour-intrinsic non-response axis; (3) highlights a responder-associated immunoglobulin / antigen-presentation programme (NMF10) together with dysfunctional-effector CD8 and Tfh-like composition features as the strongest discovery-stage immune correlates; (4) shows that the IMbrave150 CD8 signal remains treatment-arm-selective in real per-patient data; and (5) demonstrates, via a genuinely held-out scRNA-seq cohort, that cross-regimen transferability is limited and layer-specific rather than guaranteed. We release all code, tables and figures to support biomarker discovery in HCC immunotherapy at appropriately reported uncertainty.
